The Situation

A Live Show With No Retakes

What I Led Into

Confetti was Facebook's first interactive trivia game show, live on Facebook Watch, a home that reached 400M+ people a month. The raw material was cutting-edge live features. The job was to turn them into a stable, highly engaging product that could scale globally and safely, without breaking under real-time load or abuse.

The Opportunity

In live, the interaction is the product, not the video around it. The opportunity was to design the moment a viewer becomes a player, and make it reliable enough to repeat every day.

The Bet

Design for the Shared Moment

Confetti ran as a state-machine-driven experience, so every moment had to account for latency, real-time state and safety at scale. I judged each decision by one test: does this make a huge audience feel like one room, and will it still work when the room gets bigger?

Craft Decisions

Four Moves That Mattered

1. Fewer Steps Between Watching and Playing

Every extra step in the join and eligibility flow is a player lost before the first question. I cut it to what a live moment can tolerate.

2. Latency as a Design Material

We designed to a target latency under 50 milliseconds. I made deliberate latency and interaction tradeoffs: a rich interaction that arrives late is a broken one, so we favored the ones that stayed reliable under load.

3. Safety Inside the Release

Safety checks shipped with every feature instead of chasing it. With integrity teams in the room from the start, abuse handling was part of the design, not a patch.

4. Notifications as the Growth Lever

The show's survival depended on daily active viewers, so we tested notifications as an audience driver. They proved to be the biggest lever on Facebook, so effective that Facebook later had to limit how other teams could use them.

Impact & Outcome

What It Added Up To

Across all markets Confetti reached about 1M daily active players, and Mexico was the highest, with 24,000+ players per episode and coverage in Mexican and international press. At its peak, Confetti had the most daily active viewers on Facebook Watch, passing Red Table Talk, which had held that spot (Meta's internal metrics). Facebook's own 2018 numbers also named it the most-commented show on Watch. It launched in the US first, then Canada, Mexico, the UK, India, Thailand, Vietnam and the Philippines, and the US and Mexico were the strongest. Facebook wound the show down in 2019, but the reusable interaction patterns we built were adopted by other live formats, so the work outlived the show.
