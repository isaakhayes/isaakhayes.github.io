The Situation

Help Was Four Different Products

What I Inherited

Help was fragmented across Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and Oculus. Answers were hard to find, and the systems and tone were inconsistent on every surface.

Why Now

We were already relabeling the Oculus help center to Meta, so people would know the Meta brand and not the old Oculus brand. While we were doing it, the ask came: since you're redoing the Help Center, merge them all.

The Opportunity

Support is where people meet a company when something has gone wrong. One information architecture and one shared system makes four products feel like one company that is paying attention.

The Bet

Help Is One Product, Not Four

Every product team wanted its own information above the fold. Facebook, WhatsApp and the other proprietary sites each argued theirs mattered most, and some argued it outranked Oculus. My view was that no one gets the top spot, because what belongs there depends on where the person came from. So I started with structure, not screens, and tested the hypothesis.

I also moved first. At Facebook the first team to do something usually wins, so I talked to the other teams early, so they knew I was leading it.

How I Worked

Leading People and Teams.

The Team

I managed three designers, two in Singapore and one in Seattle, all more junior than me. I had just been promoted when I was asked onto the Oculus support team, because of my leadership in Singapore. I led a workshop and helped with goals. We held our team meetings in VR, in the same tools we were designing.

Leading Five Teams

The teams weren't my direct reports, so I led through the work: structure first, then screens. I did research on where to focus and talked with partner teams about the biggest issues.

More Than a Help Center

Our team handled everything after someone buys: returns, exchanges and fixes, gift cards, and over time deals and discounts. We also worked on the Meta Store launch, on the digital side. We knew about it early and held back some work so we didn't build things twice.

Where We Were Heading

A fully immersive VR Help Center. And a community Q&A, like Zendesk, where people answer each other. The plan was to combine those answers with the ones we wrote and feed them to an AI.

Craft Decisions

Three Moves That Mattered

1. Measure Resolution, Not Page Views

Search and resolution signals told us whether people actually found an answer. A busy help center is not the same as one that works.

2. Accessibility and Localization From Day One

WCAG 2.1 AA and global localization were set up front, not retrofitted. At this scale a late fix is a rebuild.

3. Governance Ships With the System

The navigation model and the governance were part of what launched. That is what reduced the maintenance burden across teams and kept the system coherent.

Results

What It Added Up To

It launched with the Meta Store experience in 2022. One coherent experience now serves 50M+ users. Testing proved the hypothesis, and a section I had argued against was cut afterward. The shared system also cut the maintenance burden across teams.

We set WCAG 2.1 AA and global localization up front, so it was built in, not bolted on.
